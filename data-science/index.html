The Analytics Revolution

From gut feelings to data-driven decisions

1970s-80s

Birth of Sabermetrics

Bill James pioneers statistical analysis, challenging traditional baseball wisdom with data.

2003

Moneyball Era

Oakland A's prove small-market teams can compete using analytics. On-base percentage becomes king.

2015

StatCast Revolution

MLB introduces tracking technology measuring exit velocity, launch angle, and sprint speed in real-time.

2020s

AI & Machine Learning

Neural networks process millions of data points, predicting outcomes with unprecedented accuracy.

What Our Models CAN'T Predict

Honesty builds trust - here's what we admit we don't know

We don't predict magic. We find mathematical edges that work over thousands of bets. Here's what remains unpredictable:

In-Game Injuries

A pitcher tears his hamstring in the 3rd inning

Impact: Completely changes game outcome mid-contest

Our Approach: Live betting models adjust, but can't predict freak injuries

Manager Decisions

Surprise lineup changes, early hooks, bullpen management

Impact: Analytics say leave pitcher in, manager panics and pulls him

Our Approach: Track tendencies, but humans are unpredictable

Player Personal Issues

Family emergencies, off-field distractions, contract disputes

Impact: Mental state affects performance in unmeasurable ways

Our Approach: Can't quantify head not in the game

The Clutch Gene

Some believe certain players perform better in big moments

Impact: Data shows clutch is mostly luck plus small sample noise

Our Approach: Doesn't exist statistically - we ignore it

Momentum & Vibes

Team has momentum or clubhouse chemistry

Impact: Narratives humans love, but data doesn't support

Our Approach: Winning streaks don't predict future performance better than talent

We embrace uncertainty. Our edge comes from being right 55-60% of the time, not 100%. Bet value, not outcomes.
